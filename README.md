# DUO — instrument collection

Sixteen browser-local instruments developed from the supplied references and interaction feedback. Signal remains the default entry point, with its saved appearance, sound and camera settings intact. Open the grid icon in the footer to switch instruments. The selector is a flat list, including the reference instruments, two preserved experiments, MilkDrop, Receiver and Roses. Direct links use `collection.html#relay` (replace `relay` with an ID below).

The app is served directly from the repository root. No build step, package installation or remote asset requests are required.

## Collection

| Instrument / URL ID | Main interaction |
| --- | --- |
| Relay / Resonator · `relay` | Pluck the metal reeds, shape their material and damping in the resonance chamber, and edit the step sequence. A six-mode resonator replaces Carrier / Modulator. |
| Cellular / Automata · `cellular` | Touch the ambient floating cell pool to gather it around a contact; release to disperse it. Paint, step, clear or reseed the right Game of Life; births and population affect the sound. CLOCK RUN follows transport, CONTACT CLOCK follows held contacts, and CLOCK PAUSED leaves the simulation stopped while living cells continue independently phased drift, breathing and contour motion. The Cellular drift appearance control sets the amount; reduced motion or zero drift freezes the visual pose. |
| Cell Census · `census` | Draw a note route through inverse lime-and-black scan fields. X controls pitch, Y controls spatial filtering, and top buttons select the oscillator. The lower EQ and spectrum retain real audio measurements. |
| Machine / Swarm · `swarm` | Edit pitch lanes and play the flow field. Scheduled notes travel down circuit traces to translucent bridges, whose arrival impulses disturb the larger, softly feathered tornado. |
| Graticule / Lissajous · `graticule` | Sonar crossings trigger star notes; drag a star outside the radar to remove it. Waveform buttons animate radar scale, and the sweep has a fading wake. The oscillator pair drives the full-container XY Lissajous figure. |
| Machine / Man · `waveform` | Create a connected, volumetric strand on the right; its arrival in the left dithered receiving zone triggers sound. |
| GeoSynth · `geo` | Select note tiles in a pannable, zoomable map. Three-dimensional colour-blended bodies cross into a wireframe wavetable and sound on arrival. Drag the target upward for table position and rightward for harmonic warp. |
| Etching / Machine · `hand` | Play the ambient 3D bead ring or chromatic keyboard. Drag the outer ring for detune/spread, and sequence prisms for pitch. Animated feeds connect the ring to the responsive machine form; large dials and gradient bars edit synthesis. |
| Structural Score · `structure` | Edit the sequence truss over a wider pitch range, move waveform blocks on the perpendicular synthesis beam, strum string chords and tilt the reflective filter disc while positioning its crosshair. Sequence packets connect to the spectrum. |
| Past / Future · `granular` | Three square-dot source windows cycle into distinct output chunks. Multitouch the elastic point cloud and satellite; shape and mix the real wavetable layer. Stable pitched grains, longer overlap and tone filtering produce a more coherent default texture. Import local audio or use the internal harmonic buffer. |
| Timbre Terrain · `terrain` | Preserved contour-terrain instrument: play the coloured note map, blend spatial timbre regions, and orbit or pinch the terrain. |
| Phase Volume · `phase-volume` | Preserved delayed phase-space volume, expanded to a single surface. Drag to change frequency ratio and modulation index; adjust sampling, depth, delay and viewing angles. |
| MilkDrop · `milkdrop` | Play scale notes across the full feedback field; touch height changes filter brightness and wavetable tone. Touches locally displace the visual flow. Select from 18 effects across eight geometries, or cycle and morph between them. |
| Receiver · `receiver` | Play the phosphor field, drag signal markers to tune their pitch and brightness, or press Play to scan between them. Detuned sidebands, analogue drift and filtered noise shape a sustained receiver voice. |
| Roses · `roses` | Touch individual 3D petals to play glass chimes. Drag empty space to orbit and pinch to zoom; Play scans petals over a sustained harmonic bed. The rose grows into view as stems, leaves and layered ASCII petals. |

Each instrument has independent sound, visual settings and editable data, stored locally. Old `#carrier` links open Relay with new defaults; the previous Carrier settings remain untouched in storage. Sound and Appearance are independent draggable panels. Appearance groups controls by the object they affect, followed by shared Layout and Postprocessing sections. Left width and right width stay anchored to their outside edges; Gap sets minimum separation. If the requested widths exceed the available space they scale proportionally. In portrait, the surfaces stack and the same shares control their heights. Relay, Receiver, Roses, Phase Volume and MilkDrop use one full-width surface.

Postprocessing applies blur followed by monochrome soft-light grain. The grain preserves black and white while texturing midtones. Shared scene bloom has been removed, including from older imported settings. Roses retains its own petal bloom and Receiver its own phosphor halation, matching their references. The footer and floating settings stay sharp. Blur defaults to zero. Shared film grain defaults to zero except for Relay. Receiver has separate CRT grain and scanline controls. Sliders support dragging after pressing anywhere on their track, including a second touch while playing. Hold the tempo readout to reveal its relative horizontal scrubber.

Save a named look, reset to Reference, undo changes, or export/import the current instrument's JSON. Untouched sound and appearance defaults from the first three releases upgrade to the current reference settings; customized settings, compositions and named looks are preserved. Legacy GeoSynth and Graticule sessions and named looks are copied into their preserved studies once, without replacing existing study settings. Exports are instrument-specific. They do not contain imported audio files: a granular source survives instrument switches within the tab, but must be reimported after a page reload. Imports accept browser-decodable audio under 64 MB and two minutes.

The collection uses Web Audio FM, modal and detuned receiver voices, sample-clock sequencing, per-contact Hold/Arp, bounded polyphony and output limiting. It shares Signal's bundled Tone.js standardized audio-context layer. Direct interaction activates audio without starting Play. Every gesture can retry activation, including the ending touch on Safari; a stalled attempt cannot block later input. A completed first tap can audition briefly if the audio context resumes within 150 ms of release. Cancelled or expired touches never replay. Signal uses the same bounded activation policy. The master connects directly to the audio destination, with metering on a parallel branch. Supported browsers use the music-playback AudioSession category. Interruption cancels the sequence, contacts and external grain scheduler, and a fresh gesture can resume audio.

Census EQ runs through real low-shelf, peaking and high-shelf filters. GeoSynth and Past / Future use finite Fourier wavetables; the displayed wave and oscillator use the same coefficients. Past / Future combines actual windowed buffer grains and wavetable oscillators in a bounded pool, routed through the same output meter. Three.js renders the bead rings, wavetable, terrain, bridges, phase volume, grain cloud and implicit blob surfaces. MilkDrop uses bounded WebGL1 feedback buffers, with a Canvas fallback when WebGL is unavailable; Canvas also draws other fields and technical annotations. Signal retains its existing synthesis engine and renderer.

For each instrument, test two-finger playing with Hold/Arp, tempo scrubbing, sound and appearance changes while a note is held, rotation/resize, and returning to a saved instrument. Pausing cancels future sequence arrivals while leaving held contacts sounding; turn Hold off to release them. Escape releases notes when no panel or disclosure is open. A cancelled touch releases its own source even with Hold enabled. Backgrounding the app or leaving an instrument silences it. A–K plays collection voices, except Past / Future, whose touch surfaces control its grain engine.

The PWA caches Signal and the complete collection. An update waits until the instrument is idle before activating. Installed iOS audio interruptions, touch callouts, system chrome and hardware frame rate still require testing on the device; desktop browser emulation does not reproduce those OS behaviours.

## Roses

Open `collection.html#roses` or select Roses in the footer. Touch a petal to strike its chime, or drag across petals to articulate different scale notes. Leaves and stems also respond with lower notes. Empty-space dragging orbits the sculpture. Pinch anywhere to zoom up to 3.2×; once the pinch starts, its midpoint pans the close view. Wheel zoom follows the cursor and Shift-drag pans. Two stationary touches remain independent notes; a deliberate pinch releases only the notes owned by those two fingers. Held notes retain their petal identity through camera changes and changes to the number of petal layers. Play visits all five flowers above a slow, overlapping three-chord foundation. Direct touch does not require Play.

The reference is the supplied Gruff “roses / sound on” clip. Curled petal sheets, compound leaves and stems are projected in 3D, with a bounded software depth buffer shared by the visible surface and petal picking. A screen-space ASCII pass chooses character density from the shaded model. Local bloom softens the luminous marks; it does not add a separate cell fill. Struck petals release small glyph fragments into world space. The entry reveal defaults to 10.5 seconds (adjustable in Motion) and builds the stem, branches, leaves, buds and finally unfolding flowers. Struck heads nod and settle while their branches follow; leaves twist and brighten, and touching the main stem gently bends the attached plant. Zoom and viewport-relative framing persist. Reduced motion shows the complete sculpture and disables ambient motion and fragments.

The native voice combines a warm harmonic body with three struck glass modes in the reference's approximate frequency ratio `1 : 2.5 : 4.5`. Each mode decays independently. Sound exposes body/glass balance, decay, detuning, drift, envelope, delay and foundation level. Appearance groups petal geometry, stems/leaves, ASCII sampling, light, motion and annotations. Camera and settings persist with the instrument; no reference audio or remote assets are bundled.

Roses validation: `python3 tests/roses.browser.py` covers actual touch output, per-petal picking, orbit/pinch, Hold/Arp, sequencer coverage and persistence. `python3 tests/roses-audio.browser.py` renders real PCM for timbre, control response, release and polyphony. `python3 tests/roses-renderer.browser.py` checks rendered appearance controls, reduced motion, reveal, pixel budgets and cleanup. These are browser checks, not native iOS performance measurements.

## Receiver

Open `collection.html#receiver` or choose Receiver in the footer. Touch the field to play; drag one of five signal markers to change its saved pitch and brightness. The bottom rail tunes a contact, and the right rail adjusts receiver bandwidth. Play scans all five markers at the selected musical interval. Hold, Arp and keyboard notes share the same voice engine.

The screen uses cached blue-grey glass, fine soft-light noise, raster lettering and localized phosphor halation. Appearance controls separate glass, phosphor, tracking marks and calibration. Mobile rendering recognizes desktop-identifying iPads and caps cadence at 60 Hz.

Receiver validation: `python3 tests/receiver.browser.py` checks direct touch, rails, scan order, Hold/Arp, dragging, saved data and cleanup. `python3 tests/receiver-audio.browser.py` checks rendered PCM, and `python3 tests/receiver-renderer.browser.py` checks the screen texture, appearance range, resize, reduced motion and cache bounds.

## MilkDrop

Open `collection.html#milkdrop` or select MilkDrop in the footer. Touch X plays scale-quantized notes over two octaves; touch Y changes the filter and wavetable. Hold, Arp and the keyboard share the collection voice engine. Play generates Drift, Pulse or Chords patterns; these do not gate direct playing. Sound controls include wavetable shape, FM, expression, envelope and delay.

The effect selector includes 18 original presets across fluid feedback, kaleidoscope, tunnel, interference, plasma, orbit, lattice and spectral forms. Previous/next, random, automatic cycling and clear-history controls sit over the field. Random folds away on narrow displays; every preset and Clear remain accessible. Geometry, feedback persistence, flow, symmetry, palette, exposure, audio response and transition duration are editable in Appearance. Effects morph without resetting sound. Named looks, undo and JSON round-trips use the existing host controls. Reduced motion stops autonomous flow and cycling, while touch and audio still respond.

The renderer uses measured output RMS, waveform and spectrum. The feedback image is transported into alternating bounded framebuffers rather than retaining unbounded frame history. Resize recreates history at the new dimensions. This is an original implementation inspired by [projectM's SDL frontend](https://github.com/projectM-visualizer/frontend-sdl-cpp), not a projectM engine port; native `.milk` preset import is not supported, and no projectM source or presets are bundled.

## Installed display and touch

The manifest requests fullscreen, falling back to standalone where the browser does not support it. Appearance → Shared → Layout also offers Enter/Exit fullscreen when the browser exposes a usable Fullscreen API. It follows browser-driven exits and does not offer a redundant action in an already-fullscreen installed window. iOS controls its own status indicators and may keep them visible; CSS cannot force them away. The app retains `viewport-fit=cover` and safe-area insets so controls avoid system overlays. Its height is measured independently from the CSS viewport: the large viewport in installed mode and the dynamic viewport in a browser. Stale visual-viewport snapshots cannot shrink the layout after app return. Rotation and resume briefly remeasure while WebKit settles; a focused text keyboard can temporarily reduce the available height, and leaving the editor or returning to the app restores it. Footer controls also have explicit horizontal and bottom clearance for rounded screen corners when the OS reports zero safe-area padding. Installed manifest changes may require the OS to refresh the installation.

Opening Sound, Appearance or the key/scale disclosure focuses a neutral header or close button. It never focuses a native dropdown automatically; Tab navigation and intentional dropdown selection remain available. Restored floating panels do not take focus.

Browser double-tap zoom is disabled across both shells. Text-entry fields use at least 16 px on touch devices to avoid WebKit focus zoom, including wide landscape displays. Native pinch zoom outside playing surfaces, panel scrolling, and instrument pinch gestures remain available. Holding an instrument control or label does not select text or open the WebKit touch callout. Text-entry fields retain selection and editing. Actual iOS magnifier and installed display behavior still need device verification; portable browser checks do not emulate the OS chrome.

Signal uses a touch-device rendering profile, including iPadOS when Safari advertises a desktop Mac user agent or a trackpad is attached. The ring draws at up to 60 Hz with a 700,000-pixel target and two-sample antialiasing where supported; the galaxy's WebGL field uses up to 500,000 pixels and the shared particle canvas up to 1,000,000. This limits backing-buffer work on large Retina screens without changing layout, particle counts, camera coordinates or audio scheduling. Galaxy labels retain their original resolution. Desktop rendering budgets remain unchanged. The ring's default framebuffer is no longer retained between presentations; image capture redraws the completed postprocessing pass on demand.

## Signal language

Signal uses the owner-supplied appearance: black plots, pink notes and magenta particles, with dense beads, broad particle bloom and grain in the ring finish. Its geometry, motion, matrix, lighting, labels and color settings match the imported Signal look. New sessions and Appearance resets use these values. Untouched revision-5 Signal sessions upgrade; customized looks, sound, sequence and camera settings are retained. Other visual presets remain available. The ring’s WebGL output and main UI panels are transparent so they do not cut off the shared lines.

- White arcs connect the filter hub to active voice locations. Accent-colored arcs originate at the sequence step that actually triggered each voice. Each arc and ring highlight follows that voice’s actual amplitude envelope, including release.
- Pointer-triggered arpeggio pulses carry their originating gesture through audio scheduling and pooled voice reuse. Their bead highlights and white arc endpoints follow that touch, including moving contacts and latched Hold notes. Keyboard and sequencer notes retain their voice-sector indicators.
- Matrix paths originate at the projected 3D galaxy crosshair and converge at the ring’s geometric center. Moving emissions are rasterized into a fixed low-resolution grid, then each lit cell is drawn as a square, circle or ASCII character with adjustable spacing, gap, exposure, contrast and cutoff. Dots support brightness-dependent size; ASCII selects character density from cell brightness. Appearance → Particles → Dot matrix → Cell style switches the treatment. ASCII exposes Character spacing and Character inset, and retains its spacing separately from the dot grid. Unlit cells stay transparent. Appearance → Particles → Dot matrix controls this treatment; Emission width and Samples per path control the source that feeds the matrix. Base exposure sets the bias; Loudness response scales it with actual post-master RMS, with a 120 ms attack and 600 ms release. Voice count does not determine exposure. Older free-particle sizes are converted once on load/import.
- Appearance → Particles → Bloom controls intensity, radius and cutoff of the soft particle-only glow. Bloom does not affect the ring, text or smooth voice curves. Appearance → Particles → Paths & motion controls broad, continuous wave speed and amplitude, particle travel speed, path count, curvature, disorder, spread, depth and release behavior. Slow waves evolve continuously while the source and destination stay anchored. Phase is integrated so changing speed does not jump the paths. The shared field sits behind all panel artwork and controls; panels retain their transparent fills.
- Crosshair movement also pulls a damped elastic field: the paths lag behind the drag, carry a disturbance toward the ring, and settle after release. Drag influence, Wave tension and Drag damping control this response. Input comes from the crosshair’s world-coordinate movement, so camera navigation and layout changes do not inject a false impulse. The matrix and both path endpoints remain anchored.
- Bright particle heads with fading wakes travel from the map crosshair into the ring core. Their rate follows BPM; doubling tempo doubles their travel speed without resetting position. Appearance → Particles → Paths & motion → Particle flow rate sets path traversals per beat: 0.25 crosses in four beats, and zero pauses travel. Ambient wave speed and elastic drag remain independent. Inside the ring viewport, its resolved scene depth places the particle layer at the depth of the ring’s geometric center: particles cover rear beads and are hidden by front beads. Matrix color and bloom bypass ring-only grading. The Canvas2D upload, depth composite and transparent WebGL canvas use premultiplied alpha consistently, keeping faint glow and saturated particles continuous at the canvas edge. The depth mask removes both color and coverage behind front beads. Ring blur and color fringe normalize sampled transparency consistently so thin orbital guides retain their palette color. The Canvas2D fallback uses the same rear-beads → particles → front-beads drawing order. UI controls and annotations remain above the particle field. When the workspace changes size, the particle GPU texture is reallocated to the new canvas dimensions so the ring composite keeps animating.
- The galaxy combines spiral arms, a spherical core, sparse foreground stars and a distant field. Individual stars twinkle gently with seeded, staggered rhythms; Appearance → Galaxy → Star twinkle sets the amount. Landmark and nebula brightness remains steady, and Freeze or reduced motion stop ambient twinkling. Distance changes star size, brightness and softness, and orbiting reveals parallax between these layers. Appearance → Galaxy → Galaxy depth stretches only the star volume; tuning coordinates and sound stay fixed. Galaxy spread scales the field around a fixed world origin, so dragging the tuning crosshair never translates the stars. Explicit Pan still translates the view. Galaxy, sequence and envelope plots share the same transparent frame, stroke, radius and horizontal alignment.
- Galaxy guides adapt to zoom: closer views reveal finer divisions, and distant views blend into coarser divisions. The guides stay anchored to world coordinates. Guide spacing remains an adjustable density multiplier for Cartesian, orbital and dotted guides.
- The matrix treatment is independently implemented using the fixed-cell image sampling approach demonstrated by [udaign/matrices](https://github.com/udaign/matrices), particularly Wallpaper, ValueAliasing and PhotoWidget. No code or dependency from that repository is bundled.
- The compact green wireframe sphere shows the phase of the real filter LFO; its orbit extent responds to LFO depth. The second, accent-colored marker shows the tempo/arpeggiator clock: it circles once per eight beats normally, and follows arp rate/direction when enabled. Rate and depth change the sound and the visual response together. Modulation controls are also available in Sound on short displays.
- The white sequence contour shows step velocity; accent-colored nodes show pitch. Shift-drag changes velocity. White envelope geometry and its annotations show the configured ADSR; the accent-colored envelope trace responds to sounding voices.
- Sound controls visibly shape the ring even at rest: cutoff opens/lights the core, resonance sharpens its relief, waveform changes petal contour, and detune separates/twists layers. ADSR draws the inner profile; delay/feedback set echo arcs; reverb widens a soft halo. Sequence pitch, velocity, length and swing shape outer markers; tempo/arp drive a clock marker; Hold adds a latch bracket. While playing, real voice envelopes still drive the lit wedges.
- Signal uses the same inclined orbital guide lines as the other appearances. Guide opacity and spacing adjust them without changing camera framing. The hidden geometry bounds still reserve space for the ring’s full rotation, maximum bead breathing and sound response, keeping the ring inside the canvas throughout the zoom range. Camera distance stays independent of orbit angle and animation phase. Adjacent voice-sector envelopes blend continuously, without dark seams between equally lit regions. Individual bead sizes breathe with seeded independent phases and slightly different periods, without a collective ring pulse; Appearance → Ring → Motion exposes Bead breathing and Breathing rate independently of the musical tempo. Reduced motion disables ambient breathing, and Freeze holds its phase.
- Appearance includes dot-matrix size/count/glow, density, field depth/spread, signal weight, response, trails, ring geometry/material, grid, palette, contrast, glow, grain, and other controls. Freeze captures the ring and shared field while the audio continues.
